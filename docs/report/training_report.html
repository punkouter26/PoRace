Generated 2026-10-08 11:50. Two creatures: the Unitree Go2 robot dog (racing now) and the Unitree G1 humanoid (body ready, not yet trained). Trained in MuJoCo Warp, played in Unity through the native MuJoCo plugin.
In one paragraph. The dog has two brains that work: one to stand, walk, turn and take hits, one to get back up. Together they race four-at-a-time on two tracks. What it cannot do yet is run: it tops out around 1.3 to 1.5 m/s. The first attempt to teach running backfired and was thrown away; a corrected second attempt is training now. The humanoid's body is in Unity and matches the training simulator, but it has no brain yet.

1. Overall score of the walking brain

Think of it as a report-card grade for one 20-second test: points for moving at the speed asked and staying level, minus points for shaking, wasting energy or falling. It sat near 18 while the dog only balanced, climbed to 26 once it learned to walk, then dropped on purpose each time the test got harder (shoves and cubes, then shoves twice as strong). A lower score on a harder test is not a worse dog; the last red dot is the brain racing today.

2. Does it go the speed it is told?

How close the dog's real speed is to the joystick command, added up over the test. For the first 70 million steps it stayed low because the dog had found a lazy answer: stand still and only turn. Then walking "clicked", and this is the curve that shows it. It dips when it also has to survive being shoved.

3. Teaching it to run

Red, attempt 1: the score went up, which looked like progress, but the dog was getting worse: it learned to stand still whenever asked to go fast. The scoring gave almost no credit for "partly fast", so refusing was the cheapest answer. Purple, attempt 2: scoring fixed so partial speed earns partial credit; it has only just started. This chart is the one to watch next.

Bonus: the stand-up brain

A separate brain that starts on its back, side or upside down. Halfway through it could right itself only from some positions; by the end it stands up from every random start in under 2.2 seconds.

Why "run fast" is red: asked vs. reached

Each group is one speed command from a standstill. The green dashed line is what was asked; the bar is what the dog actually did.

  • Blue, today's brain: follows commands well up to 1.5, manages 1.5 when asked for 2.0, and stands still when asked for 2.5 or more.
  • Red, run attempt 1: after more training it stood still at 1.5 as well. That is why it was discarded.

A bar at zero does not mean the dog fell. It stays upright and braces. In races this is why a dog bumped to a halt used to stay stuck; the race now restarts its speed command gently to get it moving again.

Honest caveats

  • No running yet. Everything in the "Run fast" column is either failing or unproven. Attempt 2 may also fail.
  • The humanoid is only a body. Its green cell means the physics match between Unity and the simulator with no brain attached. Its training environment and tests are written but have never been run.
  • Fast starts rely on a helper. The game ramps speed commands over a quarter second and un-sticks stalled racers; without those the current brain freezes on sudden fast commands.
  • One parity run drifts. At 0.5 m/s the joints of the Unity and simulator runs wander apart by 5 seconds while speed, step rhythm and effort still agree. Not yet explained.
  • Charts 1 to 3 have few dots in their latest sections because checkpoints are 23 million steps apart.
